# i3League — Curriculum Architecture Summary
### Read this first

---

## The Six Stages

Every student or team follows the same innovation journey, in order:

**INSIGHT → INVESTIGATE → IMAGINE → ITERATE → IMPACT → INFLUENCE**

| Stage | Core Question |
|---|---|
| INSIGHT | Is this a real problem? |
| INVESTIGATE | Why is it happening, and what does the evidence say? |
| IMAGINE | What could solve it, which direction should we choose, and what must we test first? |
| ITERATE | Can we build and test the critical assumption? |
| IMPACT | Did it actually make a measurable difference? |
| INFLUENCE | Can we defend it, sustain it, and convince someone to take the next step? |

There is no separate IP Checkpoint stage — originality, attribution, and IP awareness are a topic taught and evidenced inside IMAGINE.

---

## The Four Curriculum Layers

Each stage is built the same way, across four layers:

**LEARN → DO → SHOW → REVIEW**

- **LEARN** — student-facing notes for every topic (what it is, why it matters, an example, a common mistake, a takeaway, a reflection question)
- **DO** — activities and named worksheets that turn each topic into something a student actually produces
- **SHOW** — the required artifact for the stage, and exactly what must be submitted
- **REVIEW** — the rubric a reviewer uses to decide whether the submission is ready to advance

---

## The Operating Cycle

Layers describe curriculum content. The cycle below describes what happens once a student submits:

**Feedback → Revise → Resubmit → Advance**

This is not a fifth layer — it's the loop that runs on top of REVIEW. A submission that isn't ready yet gets specific feedback, the student revises and resubmits, and the cycle repeats until it advances. Revision is expected, not penalized.

---

## Depth Tags

Every topic across all six stages carries one tag:

- **CORE — ALL** — required for every student, regardless of grade
- **HS CORE** — required for high school students (often the same topic as CORE — ALL, but with more depth)
- **STRETCH** — advanced enrichment for strong or older students, or for complex projects

There is one common innovation pathway — not separate middle school and high school curricula. Depth increases by grade level, project complexity, and student readiness, not by a different topic list.

---

## The Core Principle

**Students build evidence, not just ideas.**

At every stage, advancement depends on the quality of the thinking and evidence behind the work — not on how polished, expensive, or impressive the final product looks. Four principles hold across the entire competition:

1. Evidence over hype.
2. Revision is expected, not punished.
3. An unsuccessful solution can still represent excellent innovation work if the learning is rigorous.
4. Judges evaluate the quality of the innovation journey, not simply how impressive the final product looks.
