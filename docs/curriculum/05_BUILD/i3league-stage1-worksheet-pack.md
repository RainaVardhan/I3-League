## 3. Interview Consent Script

**Before interviewing someone, say:**

> Hi, I'm working on an i3League innovation project about [general topic]. I'm trying to understand people's experiences with this problem. This is for a student project. Is it okay if I ask you a few questions? You don't have to answer anything you don't want to answer, and I won't include your name unless you give permission.

Did the person agree to participate? ☐ Yes ☐ No — *if no, do not continue the interview.*

May I take written notes? ☐ Yes ☐ No

May I use an anonymous quote from this conversation? ☐ Yes ☐ No

May I identify you by name? ☐ Yes ☐ No

**Default student rule:** When in doubt, keep the participant anonymous.

**Never ask participants for** passwords, account numbers, medical records, private financial information, or other sensitive personal information.

*(For minors interviewing minors, the full program-wide research/safety policy applies. This worksheet does not replace that policy.)*

### Scope test
- ☐ Specific enough that I know who experiences it
- ☐ Narrow enough to investigate this year
- ☐ Supported by something I have actually observed
- ☐ Supported by someone affected by it
- ☐ A problem, not a solution

### Too broad? Complete:

**Step 5 — What evidence supports this?**
Observation evidence: ________________________________
Firsthand evidence: ________________________________

### Build your first statement
> [Affected group] experiences [specific problem] when/in [context], which results in [meaningful consequence].

Draft: ________________________________

### Solution detector
Does your problem statement contain words such as: app, platform, AI system, device, website, program, tool, service, "should create," "needs a"?

If yes, ask: **Did I accidentally put my solution inside my problem?**

❌ "Students need an AI cafeteria app."
✅ "Students purchasing lunch during a 25-minute lunch period experience long checkout lines that reduce the time available to eat."

## 7. Stage 1 Evidence Check
### Are We Ready to SHOW?

**Problem**
- ☐ We can clearly state the problem
- ☐ We can identify who experiences it
- ☐ We can explain where/when it occurs
- ☐ Our problem statement does not contain our solution

**Evidence**
- ☐ We recorded 3–5 meaningful observations
- ☐ We spoke with at least one person affected by the problem
- ☐ We can show evidence supporting the problem
- ☐ We did not rely only on our own opinions

**Learning**
- ☐ We can explain something we learned
- ☐ We can identify an assumption that changed or became less certain

**Responsibility**
- ☐ Participants agreed to speak with us
- ☐ We protected private information
- ☐ Our evidence is truthful

**Team**
- ☐ We completed our Team Charter if working as a team

> **If you cannot check every required box, you are not failing. You have identified what to do next.**

---

## How the Stage 1 BUILD pieces connect

**LEARN** (Observation → Empathy → Scope → Ethical Interviewing → Teamwork)
→ **DO** (Friction Log → Empathy Interview → Scope Worksheet → Consent Script → Team Charter)
→ **SHOW** (Problem Statement Builder pulls the strongest evidence)
→ **Validated Problem Statement + Evidence Notes**
→ **REVIEW** (Six-dimension Stage 1 rubric)
→ **ADVANCE** to Stage 2 INVESTIGATE, or **REVISE & RESUBMIT**

---

## Platform design note

These worksheets should not be built as downloadable PDFs first. For the platform, they should become structured fields students fill in and save over time, with the system automatically assembling their strongest entries into the Stage 1 submission: **Do the work once → save evidence → select strongest evidence → system builds the submission** — rather than fill worksheet → copy elsewhere → make PDF → upload → reviewer reads duplicate information.
