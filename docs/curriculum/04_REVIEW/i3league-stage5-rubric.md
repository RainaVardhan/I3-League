# i3League Stage 5 — IMPACT
## Advancement Review Rubric

**Required Artifact:** Impact Report

**Stage Gate Question:** Can the team state honestly what changed, what did not, and what the evidence actually supports?

**Mental Test:** Compared to what? → What changed? → How do we know? → What didn't we prove?

**What reviewers are NOT judging at this stage:** We are judging evidence of impact, not whether the result was positive.

---

## 1. Evidence Quality

**Reviewer checks:** Is there a credible baseline, benchmark, or comparison condition? Is the result measured with appropriate quantitative and/or qualitative evidence?

| Level | Descriptor |
|---|---|
| Not Yet Evident | No baseline, benchmark, or comparison condition exists, or the result is asserted without measurement ("we finished the solution," "people liked it"). |
| Developing | A comparison point exists but is weak, estimated after the fact, or the result evidence is thin. |
| Meets Standard | A credible baseline, benchmark, or comparison condition and a measured result are both present, with quantitative and/or qualitative evidence appropriate to the project. |
| Strong Evidence | The comparison point and result are strong and well-documented, and the evidence mix (numbers plus stories, where each is appropriate) gives a fuller, more convincing picture. |

**Load-bearing requirement:** A credible baseline, benchmark, comparison condition, or other appropriate reference point must exist. Without a meaningful comparison, an impact claim cannot be evaluated.

---

## 2. Reasoning & Interpretation

**Reviewer checks:** Are alternative explanations for the result considered? Are claims proportional to what the evidence actually supports? (HS: is correlation vs. causation addressed?)

| Level | Descriptor |
|---|---|
| Not Yet Evident | Claims significantly exceed what the evidence supports, or no alternative explanation for the result was ever considered. |
| Developing | Some interpretation is offered but alternative explanations are shallow or claims are somewhat overstated. |
| Meets Standard | Claims are proportional to the evidence, and at least one credible alternative explanation is considered and addressed. |
| Strong Evidence | The interpretation is rigorous and honest — the team actively worked to rule out or account for competing explanations before making its claim. |

**Key test:** Reviewer should be able to complete: "The evidence shows ___ changed, and the most likely reason is ___ (having considered ___ as an alternative)."

---

## 3. Process Rigor

**Reviewer checks:** Was the success metric chosen because it reflects the real problem, not because it was easy to measure? Was the baseline, benchmark, or comparison condition established before or independent of the result, not reconstructed afterward?

| Level | Descriptor |
|---|---|
| Not Yet Evident | The metric doesn't meaningfully reflect the original problem, or the comparison point appears to have been estimated after seeing the result. |
| Developing | The metric is reasonable but its connection to the original problem is not clearly justified. |
| Meets Standard | The metric was chosen because it reflects the real problem, and the baseline/benchmark/comparison was established independently of the result. |
| Strong Evidence | Metric selection and comparison-point establishment are clearly and rigorously justified, showing the team thought this through before collecting the result. |

---

## 4. Integrity & Reflection

**Reviewer checks:** Are limitations honestly named? Are unintended consequences considered? Does the final conclusion (supported / partially supported / not supported) match the evidence, even if the impact was not achieved?

| Level | Descriptor |
|---|---|
| Not Yet Evident | Limitations are hidden or absent, unintended consequences are not considered, or the conclusion overstates what the evidence shows. |
| Developing | Some limitations or unintended consequences are named but are minimal or vague. |
| Meets Standard | Limitations, alternative explanations, and unintended consequences are honestly documented, and the stated conclusion matches the evidence — whatever that conclusion is. |
| Strong Evidence | The team names the specific limitation a skeptical reviewer would raise first, and its conclusion reflects genuine intellectual honesty about what was and wasn't proven. |

**Frozen principle:** A null or negative result can rate Meets Standard or higher across every dimension. The gate checks evidence and rigor, never whether the outcome was positive.

---

## 5. Completeness

**Reviewer checks — the submission includes:** impact statement; success metric + why it matters; baseline/benchmark/comparison; result; quantitative evidence *(where appropriate)*; qualitative evidence *(where appropriate)*; data interpretation; alternative explanations; responsible claims; limitations; unintended consequences; stakeholder impact; cost vs. benefit; final conclusion (supported/partially supported/not supported); (HS: adoption/uptake, scalability, leading/lagging indicators, ROI/unit economics, advanced analysis where applicable).

| Level | Descriptor |
|---|---|
| Not Yet Evident | Multiple required components are missing, including components necessary to evaluate whether the conclusion is credible. |
| Developing | Most components are present, but one or more required pieces are incomplete or insufficient. |
| Meets Standard | All required Stage 5 submission elements are present and usable by the reviewer. |
| Strong Evidence | All elements are present and clearly connected — baseline, result, interpretation, and conclusion read as one coherent argument. |

---

## 6. Communication & Clarity

**Reviewer checks:** Can the reviewer clearly see what changed, compared to what, and how it was measured?

| Level | Descriptor |
|---|---|
| Not Yet Evident | The reviewer cannot determine what was measured, what changed, or how the conclusion was reached. |
| Developing | The Impact Report is understandable but requires significant interpretation to follow the evidence to the conclusion. |
| Meets Standard | Baseline, result, interpretation, and conclusion are clearly presented and easy to follow. |
| Strong Evidence | The evidence trail from baseline to conclusion is immediately clear, including the honest treatment of limitations. |

---

## Stage 5 Gate Rule

**ADVANCE** — All six required dimensions must be Meets Standard or Strong Evidence, AND these non-negotiables must be present:
1. A credible baseline, benchmark, comparison condition, or other appropriate reference point exists
2. Limitations and alternative explanations are honestly addressed
3. The final conclusion matches the evidence — regardless of whether impact was achieved

Reviewer certifies: *ADVANCE — The team can state honestly what changed, what did not, and what the evidence actually supports.*

**REVISE & RESUBMIT** — If any dimension is Developing or Not Yet Evident, the student receives targeted feedback: strength → flagged dimension → specific action → resubmission expectation.

---

## Example Stage 5 Review

A team reports their hallway-alert solution "successfully reduced congestion," but has no baseline measurement from before the pilot, no discussion of limitations, and no mention of the unusually cold week that may have reduced hallway lingering regardless.

| Dimension | Rating | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Evidence Quality | Not Yet Evident | No baseline or comparison condition exists to compare against |
| Reasoning & Interpretation | Not Yet Evident | Weather is a plausible alternative explanation, never addressed |
| Process Rigor | Developing | A metric was chosen but not clearly tied to a pre-established baseline |
| Integrity & Reflection | Not Yet Evident | No limitations named; conclusion overstates what's actually known |
| Completeness | Not Yet Evident | Missing baseline, limitations, and alternative-explanation discussion |
| Communication & Clarity | Meets Standard | The report is clearly written |

**Overall: REVISE & RESUBMIT.** Required action: establish or reconstruct a credible baseline from before the pilot, address the weather as an alternative explanation, name at least one real limitation, and revise the conclusion to match what the evidence actually supports — even if that means "partially supported" rather than "successful."
