# i3League Stage 3 — IMAGINE
## Advancement Review Rubric

**Required Artifact:** Concept Portfolio

**Stage Gate Question:** Has the team selected a defensible concept and does it know exactly what it must learn next?

**Mental Test:** Explore → Compare → Decide → What could prove us wrong?

**What reviewers are NOT judging at this stage:** We are judging solution reasoning, not prototype polish.

---

## 1. Evidence Quality

**Reviewer checks:** Do the concepts trace back to real needs identified in Stages 1–2? Is the value proposition grounded in evidence rather than assumption?

| Level | Descriptor |
|---|---|
| Not Yet Evident | Concepts have no clear connection to the research findings or affected group from earlier stages. |
| Developing | Some connection to earlier evidence exists, but concepts are only loosely tied to actual identified needs. |
| Meets Standard | Concepts clearly trace back to needs and evidence established in Stages 1–2. |
| Strong Evidence | Every major concept element is explicitly justified by a specific piece of prior evidence or research finding. |

---

## 2. Reasoning & Interpretation

**Reviewer checks:** Was the Decision Matrix completed before the concept was selected, or after, to justify a favorite? Is the final choice actually supported by the comparison? Is the critical assumption genuinely capable of weakening or killing the concept if false?

| Level | Descriptor |
|---|---|
| Not Yet Evident | Only one real idea was explored, or the Decision Matrix appears reverse-engineered to justify a concept already chosen. The critical assumption is trivial or unfalsifiable. |
| Developing | Multiple concepts exist but comparison reasoning is thin, or the critical assumption is only loosely connected to what would actually make the concept fail. |
| Meets Standard | The Decision Matrix genuinely informed the selection, and the critical assumption is one that, if wrong, would meaningfully weaken or kill the concept. |
| Strong Evidence | The comparison reasoning is transparent and rigorous, and the critical assumption is sharply and specifically identified as the single highest-leverage thing to test next. |

**Key test:** Reviewer should be able to complete: "We chose this concept because ___, and the thing that could prove us wrong is ___." Both halves must be genuinely supported.

---

## 3. Process Rigor

**Reviewer checks:** Were at least 3 genuinely different concepts generated (not variations of one idea)? Were constraints, risk, accessibility, ethics, and AI fit (if relevant) actually considered for the concepts, not just the winner? Did selection happen only after comparison, per the required sequence?

| Level | Descriptor |
|---|---|
| Not Yet Evident | Fewer than 3 genuinely different concepts were generated, or the required sequence (Explore → Compare → Decide) was skipped — the team appears to have decided first. |
| Developing | 3 concepts exist but some are cosmetic variations of the same idea, or constraints/risk/accessibility/ethics checks were only applied superficially. |
| Meets Standard | At least 3 genuinely different concepts were explored and evaluated against constraints, risk, accessibility, ethics, and AI fit (where relevant), in the correct sequence. |
| Strong Evidence | The exploration process visibly broadened the team's thinking — the final concept benefits from ideas or considerations surfaced by the alternatives that weren't chosen. |

**Non-negotiable:** Students cannot begin with "this is the idea we're building." Alternatives must be genuinely explored first.

---

## 4. Integrity & Reflection

**Reviewer checks:** Is originality honestly disclosed — what's original vs. adapted vs. inspired by existing work? Is attribution given where due?

| Level | Descriptor |
|---|---|
| Not Yet Evident | An adapted or borrowed concept is presented as entirely original, with no disclosure of inspiration or existing work. |
| Developing | Some acknowledgment of existing work exists but is incomplete or vague about what was adapted versus created. |
| Meets Standard | The team honestly documents what already exists, what was adapted, what is original, and any attribution needed. |
| Strong Evidence | The originality record is specific and thoughtful, clearly distinguishing inspiration, adaptation, and original contribution point by point. |

---

## 5. Completeness

**Reviewer checks — the submission includes:** value proposition; user needs; 3+ genuinely different concepts; constraints; key risks; accessibility considerations; ethical/responsible-design considerations; AI Fit evaluation (if AI proposed); major assumptions; Originality, Attribution & IP record; Decision Matrix; selected concept; reason for selection; named Critical Assumption; (HS: Build vs. Buy vs. Partner analysis).

| Level | Descriptor |
|---|---|
| Not Yet Evident | Multiple required components are missing, including components necessary to justify the selection. |
| Developing | Most components are present, but one or more required pieces are incomplete or insufficient. |
| Meets Standard | All required Stage 3 submission elements are present and usable by the reviewer. |
| Strong Evidence | All elements are present and clearly connected — the value proposition, comparison, and critical assumption read as one coherent argument. |

---

## 6. Communication & Clarity

**Reviewer checks:** Can the reviewer follow why this concept was chosen over the alternatives? Is the Decision Matrix easy to interpret?

| Level | Descriptor |
|---|---|
| Not Yet Evident | The reviewer cannot determine why the selected concept was chosen over the alternatives. |
| Developing | The comparison is present but requires significant interpretation to understand the selection logic. |
| Meets Standard | The concepts, comparison, and selection reasoning are clearly presented and easy to follow. |
| Strong Evidence | The Concept Portfolio makes the entire reasoning trail — from needs to concepts to selection to critical assumption — immediately clear. |

---

## Stage 3 Gate Rule

**ADVANCE** — All six required dimensions must be Meets Standard or Strong Evidence, AND these non-negotiables must be present:
1. At least 3 genuinely different concepts were explored
2. Selection followed, not preceded, the Decision Matrix
3. A specific, non-trivial Critical Assumption is named

Reviewer certifies: *ADVANCE — The team has selected a defensible concept and knows exactly what it must learn next.*

**REVISE & RESUBMIT** — If any dimension is Developing or Not Yet Evident, the student receives targeted feedback: strength → flagged dimension → specific action → resubmission expectation.

---

## Example Stage 3 Review

A team submits three concepts that are all versions of a mobile app (different color schemes and names), a Decision Matrix that scores their favorite highest on every criterion, and a critical assumption of "people will like the app," which is broad and hard to test meaningfully.

| Dimension | Rating | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Evidence Quality | Meets Standard | Concepts do trace to the Stage 2 problem |
| Reasoning & Interpretation | Not Yet Evident | Critical assumption is too vague to be falsifiable |
| Process Rigor | Not Yet Evident | The three concepts are cosmetic variations, not genuinely different approaches |
| Integrity & Reflection | Meets Standard | Inspiration is disclosed appropriately |
| Completeness | Meets Standard | All required sections are present |
| Communication & Clarity | Meets Standard | Portfolio is clearly written |

**Overall: REVISE & RESUBMIT.** Required action: generate at least two more genuinely different solution approaches (not app variations), rerun the Decision Matrix honestly across all of them, and rewrite the critical assumption to name something specific and testable — e.g., "Students will actually check the app before choosing a hallway route."
