# i3League Stage 4 — ITERATE
## Advancement Review Rubric

**Required Artifact:** Tested Prototype + Test Plan + Iteration Log

**Stage Gate Question:** Has the team demonstrated a real evidence-driven iteration cycle?

**Mental Test:** Assumption → Build → Test → Learn → Change → Retest

**What reviewers are NOT judging at this stage:** We are judging testing and iteration, not final-product completeness.

---

## 1. Evidence Quality

**Reviewer checks:** Is there an actual test tied to the critical assumption from Stage 3? Are observation and opinion recorded separately, not blended?

| Level | Descriptor |
|---|---|
| Not Yet Evident | No real test occurred, or the test has no clear connection to the critical assumption. Only vague impressions ("people liked it") are offered. |
| Developing | A test occurred but is loosely connected to the critical assumption, or observation and opinion are blended together without distinction. |
| Meets Standard | A real test directly targets the critical assumption, with observed behavior and reported opinion recorded separately. |
| Strong Evidence | The test evidence is rich enough to clearly support or challenge the critical assumption, with behavior and opinion cross-referenced meaningfully. |

**Load-bearing requirement:** A real test tied to the critical assumption must exist. Positive impressions alone cannot substitute for it.

---

## 2. Reasoning & Interpretation

**Reviewer checks:** Was success defined before testing, not after? Does the learning drawn from the test logically follow from what was observed?

| Level | Descriptor |
|---|---|
| Not Yet Evident | Success criteria were written after seeing the results (or not written at all), or the stated learning doesn't logically follow from what was observed. |
| Developing | Success criteria exist but are vague, or the connection between test results and stated learning is unclear. |
| Meets Standard | Success criteria were clearly defined before testing, and the learning drawn from results is logically supported by what was observed. |
| Strong Evidence | The pre-defined success criteria were specific and measurable, and the team's interpretation of results shows nuanced, honest reasoning — including when results were mixed. |

**Key test:** Reviewer should be able to confirm the success criteria were written before the test happened, not reconstructed afterward.

---

## 3. Process Rigor

**Reviewer checks:** Was the prototype fidelity appropriate for what was being tested? Was the revised version actually retested? For HS: were formal variables/controls identified where appropriate?

| Level | Descriptor |
|---|---|
| Not Yet Evident | Testing was informal with no structured plan, or a revision was made but never retested. |
| Developing | A test plan exists but fidelity choice is poorly justified, or the retest is weak or incomplete. |
| Meets Standard | Prototype fidelity was appropriately matched to the test question, and the revised version was genuinely retested. |
| Strong Evidence | The fidelity choice, test design, and retest form a tight, well-reasoned loop — each step clearly informed by the one before it. |

**Non-negotiable:** Test → Learn → Revise → Retest. A revision that was never retested does not complete the loop.

**Retest means a second appropriate validation cycle after revision.** Depending on the project, this may involve users, experiments, simulations, models, technical tests, field tests, or another credible validation method — it does not always mean finding another group of people to test on.

---

## 4. Integrity & Reflection

**Reviewer checks:** Are failed, partial, surprising, or inconclusive results reported honestly, not filtered out? Is at least one meaningful learning documented?

| Level | Descriptor |
|---|---|
| Not Yet Evident | Only positive feedback is recorded; failures or inconclusive results are omitted or minimized. |
| Developing | Some acknowledgment of imperfect results exists but is thin or defensive. |
| Meets Standard | The team honestly records failed, partial, surprising, or inconclusive findings and documents at least one meaningful learning from them. |
| Strong Evidence | The team treats an unexpected or negative result as valuable evidence and explains clearly how it shaped the next revision. |

Per the frozen principle: students are never required to manufacture a failure — but whatever the outcome was, it must be reported honestly.

---

## 5. Completeness

**Reviewer checks — the submission includes:** critical assumption being tested; MVP/Minimum Testable Version; fidelity justification; test question; success criteria (pre-testing); test participants/context; permissions where necessary; observations; participant feedback; failed/partial/surprising/inconclusive findings; what was learned; V1→V2 changes; reason for each revision; retest evidence; (HS: Formal Experiment Design; technical: Git/README).

| Level | Descriptor |
|---|---|
| Not Yet Evident | Multiple required components are missing, including components necessary to demonstrate a real iteration cycle. |
| Developing | Most components are present, but one or more required pieces are incomplete or insufficient. |
| Meets Standard | All required Stage 4 submission elements are present and usable by the reviewer. |
| Strong Evidence | All elements are present and clearly connected — the reviewer can follow the V1 → V2 learning trail without needing clarification. |

---

## 6. Communication & Clarity

**Reviewer checks:** Can the reviewer follow the version history and understand why each change was made?

| Level | Descriptor |
|---|---|
| Not Yet Evident | The reviewer cannot reconstruct what changed between versions or why. |
| Developing | The version history is present but requires significant interpretation to follow. |
| Meets Standard | The Iteration Log clearly shows what changed at each version and why. |
| Strong Evidence | The full test → learn → revise → retest trail is immediately clear and easy to follow from start to finish. |

---

## Stage 4 Gate Rule

**ADVANCE** — All six required dimensions must be Meets Standard or Strong Evidence, AND these non-negotiables must be present:
1. A real test directly tied to the critical assumption
2. Success criteria defined before testing
3. Evidence that a revised version was retested

Reviewer certifies: *ADVANCE — The team has demonstrated a real evidence-driven iteration cycle.*

**REVISE & RESUBMIT** — If any dimension is Developing or Not Yet Evident, the student receives targeted feedback: strength → flagged dimension → specific action → resubmission expectation.

---

## Example Stage 4 Review

A team built a working prototype, showed it to five friends who said they liked it, made one small visual change, and submitted.

| Dimension | Rating | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Evidence Quality | Not Yet Evident | No behavioral test of the critical assumption — only casual opinions |
| Reasoning & Interpretation | Not Yet Evident | No success criteria were defined before showing it around |
| Process Rigor | Not Yet Evident | No retest occurred after the change |
| Integrity & Reflection | Developing | Only positive feedback reported, nothing surprising or challenging noted |
| Completeness | Not Yet Evident | Missing test question, success criteria, and retest evidence |
| Communication & Clarity | Meets Standard | What was done is clearly described |

**Overall: REVISE & RESUBMIT.** Required action: write a test question and measurable success criteria tied to the critical assumption before testing again; run an actual test where you observe behavior, not just collect opinions; make a revision based on what you learn; and retest the revised version.
