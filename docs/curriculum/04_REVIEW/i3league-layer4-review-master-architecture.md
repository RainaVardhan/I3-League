# i3League — Layer 4: REVIEW
## Master Review Architecture

This defines the common review model used at every stage gate, before any stage-specific rubric is written. Every stage rubric (Stage 1 first) will be built as an instance of this architecture, not a new system each time.

---

## Review Philosophy

Review exists to protect the four frozen principles:
1. Evidence over hype.
2. Revision is expected, not punished.
3. An unsuccessful solution can still represent excellent innovation work if the learning is rigorous.
4. Judges evaluate the quality of the innovation journey, not how impressive the final product looks.

A review is a **gate check**, not a grade. Its job is to answer one question: *has this student earned the right to move to the next stage, based on the quality of their thinking and evidence?*

---

## Two Distinct Scoring Contexts

i3League needs two different uses of review, and they should not be conflated:

### 1. Advancement Review (all six stages, every student/team)
A **pass/revise gate**. Used to decide whether a submission is ready to move to the next stage. Not comparative — a team is never competing against another team here. Every team that meets the standard advances.

### 2. Finals Selection Scoring (Stage 6 only, competitive placement)
Only teams that have already advanced through all six stages enter this layer. Here, the same rubric dimensions are used **comparatively** to rank teams for Finals placement, since a real competition needs to select among multiple strong teams.

This separation keeps the learning journey (Stages 1–5, and the Stage 6 gate itself) free of competitive pressure, while still allowing Finals to be a real competition among qualified teams.

**Note:** Whether the six dimensions are weighted equally at Finals, or some (e.g., Evidence Quality, Reasoning & Interpretation, Process Rigor) carry more weight than others, is intentionally left undecided here. That belongs in a separate future document — an *i3League Finals Judging Framework* — rather than inside these developmental advancement rubrics, which exist purely as fixed-standard gates.

## Common Rubric Dimensions

Every stage rubric — regardless of stage-specific content — is built from the same six dimensions. Each stage's specific reviewer checks (already defined in Layer 3) map onto these:

| Dimension | What it checks |
|---|---|
| **Evidence Quality** | Is the evidence real, sufficient, appropriately sourced, and does it actually support the claims made? |
| **Reasoning & Interpretation** | Are the conclusions logically connected to the evidence, without overreach? |
| **Process Rigor** | Did the student follow the required method or sequence for this stage (e.g., decision matrix before selection, retest after revision)? |
| **Integrity & Reflection** | Is the student honest about limitations, failures, contradicting evidence, and what remains uncertain? |
| **Completeness** | Are all required submission contents present, per the Layer 3 requirements? |
| **Communication & Clarity** | Can a reviewer actually follow and understand the submission without extra explanation? (Stage 6 places greater emphasis on audience-facing clarity, but advancement remains floor-based and unweighted.) |

Every stage-specific reviewer check from Layer 3 belongs to one of these six dimensions. This keeps every stage's rubric structurally identical, even though the content is different.

---

## Scoring Scale (used within each dimension)

A common 4-level scale, used consistently across all six stages:

| Level | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Not Yet Evident** | The dimension is missing, or evidence contradicts the claim |
| **Developing** | Present but weak, incomplete, or partially supported |
| **Meets Standard** | Solid, credible, appropriately supported for this stage |
| **Strong Evidence** | Rigorous, well-supported, and reflective beyond the minimum bar |

**Not Yet Evident** and **Developing** are both "not ready" — the distinction just helps reviewers give useful feedback, it doesn't change the gate outcome.

---

## Advancement Rule (applies at every stage gate)

> **ADVANCE:** Every required dimension is Meets Standard or Strong Evidence.
> **REVISE & RESUBMIT:** One or more required dimensions are Developing or Not Yet Evident.

This is a floor-based gate, not an averaged score — a team cannot compensate for a genuinely missing critical assumption (Stage 3) or a missing retest (Stage 4) by being strong elsewhere. Each stage's Layer 3 "REVISE when" / "ADVANCE when" language defines which dimensions are load-bearing for that stage.

Per the frozen principles: a **null or negative result** (most relevant in Stage 5) can still rate *Meets Standard* or higher across all dimensions — the gate checks evidence and rigor, never whether the outcome was positive.

---

## Reviewer Feedback Format

Every review — regardless of stage — is returned to the student/team in the same structure:

1. **What's working** — one to three specific strengths, tied to a dimension
2. **What needs revision** — each flagged dimension, with the specific reviewer-check question that wasn't met
3. **Specific action needed** — a concrete next step, not just "improve this"
4. **Resubmission expectation** — what the reviewer needs to see to move the dimension to *Meets Standard*

Feedback is always tied to a dimension and a specific check — never a vague overall impression. This mirrors the Stage 4 principle that feedback must connect to a specific, actionable change.

---

## Revise & Resubmit Process

- A team may revise and resubmit as many times as the program calendar allows before the stage deadline — resubmission is expected, not penalized.
- Each resubmission must explicitly address the prior feedback: what changed, and why, for each flagged dimension (mirroring the Stage 4 Feedback → Revision practice students already learned).
- A resubmission that doesn't address prior feedback is returned unreviewed with a note to address the outstanding items first — reviewers don't re-score work that hasn't changed.
- There is no penalty, mark, or reduced standing attached to needing a revision. The number of revision cycles is not tracked as a negative signal — only the final submission quality matters for advancement.

---

## Team vs. Individual Considerations

Because Stage 6 requires that **every team member can explain the project and their own contribution**, reviewers at that stage add one additional check that cuts across all six dimensions: a brief individual accountability check (each member answers 1–2 questions about their role and one project decision). This is scored as part of **Integrity & Reflection**, not as a separate dimension, so it doesn't distort the six-dimension structure.
