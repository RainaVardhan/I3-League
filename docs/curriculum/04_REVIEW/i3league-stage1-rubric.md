# i3League Stage 1 — INSIGHT
## Advancement Review Rubric

**Required Artifact:** Validated Problem Statement + Evidence Notes

**Stage Gate Question:** Is this problem sufficiently validated to justify deeper investigation?

**Mental Test:** Real → Specific → Human-centered → Firsthand-evidenced → Investigable

**What reviewers are NOT judging at this stage:** Reviewers are not judging how innovative, technical, profitable, sophisticated, or impressive the future solution might be. Stage 1 judges the problem and the evidence behind it.

---

## 1. Evidence Quality

**Reviewer checks:** Is there real firsthand evidence? Does someone other than the student/team actually experience the problem? Do the observations support the stated problem? Is the evidence relevant rather than merely interesting?

| Level | Descriptor |
|---|---|
| Not Yet Evident | Problem is based mostly on assumptions or personal opinion. No meaningful firsthand evidence is provided, or the evidence does not support the problem being claimed. |
| Developing | Some firsthand evidence exists, but it is thin, vague, weakly connected to the problem, or comes from too narrow a perspective to support the current statement. |
| Meets Standard | Student provides credible firsthand evidence and meaningful observations from people actually affected by the problem. The evidence clearly supports the problem statement. |
| Strong Evidence | Multiple relevant observations or perspectives reinforce the problem while also showing nuance, variation, or something the student did not initially expect. |

**Load-bearing requirement:** Firsthand evidence must exist. No amount of writing quality can compensate for its absence.

---

## 2. Reasoning & Interpretation

**Reviewer checks:** Does the problem statement logically follow from the evidence? Is the student distinguishing what they observed from what they assumed? Is the problem framed without prematurely prescribing a solution? Can the student explain why the problem matters?

| Level | Descriptor |
|---|---|
| Not Yet Evident | The claimed problem does not logically follow from the evidence, or the "problem" is actually a proposed solution such as "we need an app." |
| Developing | The student identifies a plausible problem, but the connection between evidence and conclusion is incomplete, overgeneralized, or contains significant assumptions. |
| Meets Standard | The student makes a clear, reasonable problem claim that follows from the evidence and explains why it matters without jumping to a solution. |
| Strong Evidence | The student distinguishes assumptions from findings, explains how evidence shaped the problem framing, and demonstrates nuanced understanding of the affected person's experience. |

**Key test:** Reviewer should be able to complete: "The evidence shows that ___ experiences ___ because/when ___." If that sentence cannot be supported, the reasoning is not ready.

---

## 3. Process Rigor

**Reviewer checks:** Did the student actually observe before solving? Did they engage with someone affected by the problem? Did they narrow the scope using who, where, and when? Was interviewing conducted responsibly? For teams, was a Team Charter created?

| Level | Descriptor |
|---|---|
| Not Yet Evident | Major required Stage 1 steps were skipped. Student jumped directly to a solution or relied almost entirely on their own assumptions. |
| Developing | Some required steps were completed, but observation, interviewing, scope narrowing, or team setup was incomplete or superficial. |
| Meets Standard | Student followed the Stage 1 process: observed, engaged with an affected person, narrowed the problem appropriately, and documented team expectations where applicable. |
| Strong Evidence | Student followed the process thoughtfully and used what they learned at one step to improve the next, showing that the process genuinely influenced their thinking. |

This is where i3League reinforces: **Notice before you solve.**

---

## 4. Integrity & Reflection

**Reviewer checks:** Did the student acknowledge assumptions? Did anything they heard or observed challenge their original thinking? Did they report evidence honestly rather than only selecting information that supports their preferred problem? Were consent and privacy expectations respected?

| Level | Descriptor |
|---|---|
| Not Yet Evident | Student presents assumptions as facts, misrepresents participant input, ignores consent/privacy expectations, or gives no evidence of reflection. |
| Developing | Student acknowledges some uncertainty but reflection is superficial, or they struggle to distinguish their interpretation from what participants actually said. |
| Meets Standard | Student honestly distinguishes assumptions from evidence, identifies at least one learning or changed understanding, and handles participant information responsibly. |
| Strong Evidence | Student openly identifies a meaningful assumption that was challenged or changed and explains how that affected the final problem framing. |

Key question: What did you believe before, and what did you learn after talking to someone? This starts the Reflection thread in Stage 1 instead of introducing reflection suddenly at the end.

---

## 5. Completeness

**Reviewer checks — the submission includes:** final problem statement; affected group; why the problem matters; 3–5 meaningful observations; at least one firsthand interview/conversation; something learned from the affected person; assumption changed or challenged; who/where/when scope; Team Charter when applicable; responsible evidence/consent handling.

| Level | Descriptor |
|---|---|
| Not Yet Evident | Multiple required components are missing, including one or more components necessary to validate the problem. |
| Developing | Most components are present, but one or more required pieces are incomplete, unclear, or insufficient. |
| Meets Standard | All required Stage 1 submission elements are present and usable by the reviewer. |
| Strong Evidence | All required elements are complete, well integrated, and reinforce one another rather than appearing as disconnected requirements. |

Important: Strong Evidence should never mean "more pages." It means stronger integration and evidence.

---

## 6. Communication & Clarity

**Reviewer checks:** Can the reviewer quickly understand the problem? Is it clear who experiences it? Is the evidence organized logically? Is the submission understandable without the student having to explain missing information verbally?

| Level | Descriptor |
|---|---|
| Not Yet Evident | Submission is so unclear or disorganized that the reviewer cannot determine the problem, affected group, or supporting evidence. |
| Developing | Core ideas are understandable but require significant interpretation or clarification from the reviewer. |
| Meets Standard | Problem, affected group, evidence, and reasoning are clearly presented and easy to follow. |
| Strong Evidence | Submission is concise, coherent, and makes the evidence trail immediately understandable without unnecessary material. |

Notice that Stage 1 does not reward fancy design. A plain document can earn Strong Evidence.

---

## Stage 1 Gate Rule

**ADVANCE** — All six required dimensions must be Meets Standard or Strong Evidence, AND these three non-negotiables must be present:
1. A real affected person/group
2. Firsthand evidence
3. A problem statement that does not contain the solution

Reviewer certifies: *ADVANCE — The problem is sufficiently validated to justify deeper investigation.*

**REVISE & RESUBMIT** — If any dimension is Developing or Not Yet Evident, the student receives targeted feedback following the standard format: strength → flagged dimension → specific action → resubmission expectation.

Example (Evidence Quality — Developing): "You identified students as the affected group, but your evidence currently comes only from your own observations. Speak with at least one student who experiences the problem and document what you learned." Not: "Need more research."

---

## Example Stage 1 Review

A student submits: "Students need an AI app to reduce cafeteria wait times." They observed long lines for three days and interviewed one student.

| Dimension | Rating | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Evidence Quality | Meets Standard | Real observations + firsthand student evidence |
| Reasoning & Interpretation | Developing | Problem statement already assumes an AI app is the answer |
| Process Rigor | Meets Standard | Observation and interview completed |
| Integrity & Reflection | Meets Standard | Student documents that one original assumption changed |
| Completeness | Meets Standard | Required evidence is present |
| Communication & Clarity | Meets Standard | Submission is understandable |

**Overall: REVISE & RESUBMIT** — even though 5/6 dimensions meet the standard. This is not averaging; Reasoning is below the floor.

Required revision: Rewrite the problem without embedding a solution. Instead of "Students need an AI app to reduce cafeteria wait times," describe the evidence-supported problem itself: "Students waiting for lunch during the 25-minute lunch period experience lines averaging X minutes, reducing the time available to eat." Then resubmit.
