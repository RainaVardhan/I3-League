# i3League Stage 2 — INVESTIGATE
## Advancement Review Rubric

**Required Artifact:** Research Brief

**Stage Gate Question:** Does the team have enough credible evidence to explain what is happening, why it is happening, and what existing approaches currently miss?

**Mental Test:** Question → Evidence → Triangulate → Root Cause → Gap

**What reviewers are NOT judging at this stage:** We are judging understanding, not solution quality.

---

## 1. Evidence Quality

**Reviewer checks:** Are multiple evidence types represented? Are sources credible? Is at least one important claim triangulated across evidence types?

| Level | Descriptor |
|---|---|
| Not Yet Evident | Research relies on a single source or source type ("we asked some people and they agreed"), or sources are not credible. |
| Developing | More than one source exists, but evidence types are weak, thin, or not meaningfully triangulated. |
| Meets Standard | Multiple credible evidence types are present (e.g., interview + survey + credible secondary source), and at least one key claim is triangulated. |
| Strong Evidence | Evidence is rich, well-triangulated, and drawn from sources well-matched to the specific claims being made. |

**Load-bearing requirement:** More than one type of evidence must exist. A single source type, however well executed, cannot pass this dimension.

---

## 2. Reasoning & Interpretation

**Reviewer checks:** Does the proposed root cause follow from the evidence, rather than describing only a symptom? Do existing solutions actually leave the stated gap? Was bias considered?

| Level | Descriptor |
|---|---|
| Not Yet Evident | The stated root cause is unsupported by evidence, or is actually a symptom rather than a cause. Gap analysis ignores obvious existing solutions. |
| Developing | A root cause is proposed but the reasoning connecting evidence to cause is incomplete, or the gap analysis is shallow. |
| Meets Standard | The root cause is a reasonable, evidence-supported explanation (not just a symptom), and the gap analysis credibly shows what existing solutions miss. |
| Strong Evidence | The root cause explanation accounts for the evidence with nuance, and the gap analysis meaningfully positions the problem relative to what already exists. |

**Key test:** Reviewer should be able to complete: "The evidence suggests this is happening because ___, and existing approaches miss ___." If either half can't be supported, reasoning isn't ready.

---

## 3. Process Rigor

**Reviewer checks:** Were focused research questions defined before collecting evidence? Was root cause analysis actually conducted (e.g., Five Whys), not just asserted? Was a stakeholder map completed? (HS: was a system map completed?)

| Level | Descriptor |
|---|---|
| Not Yet Evident | Research proceeded without focused questions, or root cause / gap analysis / stakeholder mapping was skipped entirely. |
| Developing | Some process steps were completed, but questions were unfocused, root cause analysis was superficial, or stakeholder mapping was thin. |
| Meets Standard | Student followed the Stage 2 process: focused research questions, structured root cause analysis, existing-solutions comparison, and stakeholder mapping. |
| Strong Evidence | Each process step visibly informed the next — for example, an interview finding reshaped a research question, or a root cause reshaped the gap analysis. |

This is where i3League reinforces: **Good research can change your mind.**

---

## 4. Integrity & Reflection

**Reviewer checks:** Is contradicting or complicating evidence acknowledged rather than dropped? Are research limitations named? Is bias in survey/interview design addressed?

| Level | Descriptor |
|---|---|
| Not Yet Evident | Evidence that contradicts the student's preferred narrative is omitted or ignored. No limitations are named. |
| Developing | Some acknowledgment of limitations or contradicting evidence exists but is minimal or vague. |
| Meets Standard | The student honestly reports at least one piece of evidence that complicated or challenged their thinking, and names real research limitations. |
| Strong Evidence | The student explains how a specific piece of contradicting evidence changed or refined their understanding of the problem. |

---

## 5. Completeness

**Reviewer checks — the submission includes:** 2–4 focused research questions; firsthand and secondary research; source list; source credibility evaluation; multiple evidence types; contradicting evidence; triangulation of at least one claim; root cause analysis; existing solutions & gap analysis; stakeholder map; research limitations; (HS: system map).

| Level | Descriptor |
|---|---|
| Not Yet Evident | Multiple required components are missing, including components necessary to establish a credible root cause or gap. |
| Developing | Most components are present, but one or more required pieces are incomplete or insufficient. |
| Meets Standard | All required Stage 2 submission elements are present and usable by the reviewer. |
| Strong Evidence | All required elements are present and clearly build on one another — the research questions, evidence, and root cause read as one coherent investigation. |

---

## 6. Communication & Clarity

**Reviewer checks:** Can the reviewer follow the research questions through to the root cause and gap without needing clarification? Is the Research Brief organized logically?

| Level | Descriptor |
|---|---|
| Not Yet Evident | The Research Brief is disorganized enough that the reviewer cannot determine what was found or how it connects to the problem. |
| Developing | Core findings are understandable but require significant interpretation from the reviewer. |
| Meets Standard | Research questions, evidence, root cause, and gap are clearly presented and easy to follow. |
| Strong Evidence | The Research Brief reads as a clear, coherent investigation — a reviewer can trace the logic end to end without help. |

---

## Stage 2 Gate Rule

**ADVANCE** — All six required dimensions must be Meets Standard or Strong Evidence, AND these non-negotiables must be present:
1. Multiple types of evidence
2. An evidence-supported root cause (not a symptom)
3. A credible existing-solutions gap

Reviewer certifies: *ADVANCE — The team has enough credible evidence to explain what is happening, why it is happening, and what existing approaches currently miss.*

**REVISE & RESUBMIT** — If any dimension is Developing or Not Yet Evident, the student receives targeted feedback: strength → flagged dimension → specific action → resubmission expectation.

---

## Example Stage 2 Review

A team submits a Research Brief built entirely on one survey of 12 classmates, concluding students are stressed because of homework load, with no root cause analysis and no mention of existing school wellness programs.

| Dimension | Rating | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Evidence Quality | Not Yet Evident | Single evidence type, small unrepresentative sample |
| Reasoning & Interpretation | Developing | "Homework load" is closer to a symptom than a root cause |
| Process Rigor | Developing | No structured root cause process, no stakeholder map |
| Integrity & Reflection | Developing | No limitations or contradicting evidence noted |
| Completeness | Not Yet Evident | Missing root cause analysis, gap analysis, stakeholder map |
| Communication & Clarity | Meets Standard | Brief is clearly written |

**Overall: REVISE & RESUBMIT.** Required action: add at least one additional evidence type (interview or credible secondary source), run a Five Whys on the stress finding to identify a genuine root cause, and compare against at least two existing wellness approaches to identify the real gap.
