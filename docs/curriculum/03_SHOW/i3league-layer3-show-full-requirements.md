# i3League — Layer 3: SHOW
## Exact Submission & Review Requirements

**Governing rule:** Worksheets remain working documents. Students pull the strongest evidence into one polished stage submission — they do not submit every worksheet as a separate artifact.

---

## Stage 1 — INSIGHT

**Required Artifact:** Validated Problem Statement + Evidence Notes

**Submission format:** One polished Stage 1 evidence package, roughly 2–4 pages or equivalent digital format.

**Must include:**
- Final problem statement
- Clearly identified affected group
- Why the problem matters
- 3–5 meaningful observations
- At least one firsthand interview/conversation
- Something learned from the affected person
- Any assumption that changed
- Scope: who, where, when
- Team Charter for team projects
- Evidence that consent/privacy expectations were followed

**Minimum evidence standard:** The student must show that someone other than the student/team actually experiences the problem. At minimum: firsthand evidence, real observations, a specific affected group, a problem narrow enough to investigate, no solution hidden inside the problem statement.

**Reviewer checks:**
1. Is this actually a problem?
2. Who experiences it?
3. How does the student know?
4. Is there firsthand evidence?
5. Does the evidence support the stated problem?
6. Is the scope manageable?
7. Did the student challenge any assumptions?
8. Was evidence gathered responsibly?

**REVISE when:** the problem is based mainly on assumptions, is too broad, is actually a proposed solution, lacks firsthand evidence, or is not supported by the evidence presented.

**ADVANCE when:** the problem is sufficiently validated to justify deeper investigation.

**Mental test:** Real → Specific → Human-centered → Firsthand-evidenced → Investigable

## Stage 2 — INVESTIGATE

**Required Artifact:** Research Brief

**Submission format:** One polished Research Brief, approximately 4–8 pages or equivalent digital format, depending on project complexity.

**Must include:**
- 2–4 focused research questions
- Firsthand research
- Relevant secondary research
- Source list
- Source credibility evaluation
- More than one type of evidence
- Evidence that challenges or complicates the student's thinking
- Triangulation of at least one important claim
- Root Cause Analysis
- Existing Solutions & Gap Analysis
- Stakeholder Map
- Research limitations
- HS: System Map

**Minimum evidence standard:** Students should not be able to advance based on "we asked some people and they agreed." They need multiple forms of evidence appropriate to the project — e.g., interview + survey + credible secondary source, or observation + expert interview + published data.

**Reviewer checks:**
1. Are the research questions focused?
2. Are multiple evidence types represented?
3. Are sources credible?
4. Was bias considered?
5. Is contradictory evidence acknowledged?
6. Does the proposed root cause follow from the evidence?
7. Is the team describing a root cause rather than only a symptom?
8. Do existing solutions actually leave the stated gap?
9. Are the correct stakeholders identified?
10. Did the research genuinely change or strengthen the team's understanding?

**REVISE when:** research relies on one source type, sources are weak, conclusions exceed the evidence, the stated root cause is unsupported, or the gap analysis ignores obvious existing solutions.

**ADVANCE when:** the team has enough credible evidence to explain what is happening, why it is happening, and what existing approaches currently miss.

**Mental test:** Question → Evidence → Triangulate → Root Cause → Gap

## Stage 3 — IMAGINE

**Required Artifact:** Concept Portfolio

**Submission format:** One Concept Portfolio, approximately 4–8 pages or equivalent visual/digital format.

**Must include:**
- Value proposition
- User needs
- At least 3 genuinely different concepts
- Constraints considered
- Key risks
- Accessibility considerations
- Ethical/responsible-design considerations
- AI Fit evaluation if AI is proposed
- Major assumptions
- Originality, Attribution & IP record
- Decision Matrix
- Selected concept
- Reason for selection
- One clearly named Critical Assumption
- HS: Build vs. Buy vs. Partner analysis

**Minimum evidence standard:** Students cannot begin with "this is the idea we're building" — they must demonstrate they explored alternatives first. Required sequence: Explore → Compare → Check Responsibility/Originality → Decide → Identify Critical Assumption.

**Reviewer checks:**
1. Were genuinely different solutions considered?
2. Do the concepts trace back to user needs?
3. Were real constraints considered?
4. Were risks considered honestly?
5. Is the solution inclusive and responsible?
6. If AI is used, does it actually add value?
7. Does the student acknowledge existing work/inspiration?
8. Was the Decision Matrix completed before selection?
9. Is the final choice supported by the comparison?
10. Is the critical assumption actually capable of killing or significantly weakening the concept?

**REVISE when:** only one real idea was explored, alternatives are cosmetic variations, the Decision Matrix was reverse-engineered to justify a favorite idea, originality is overstated, or the critical assumption is trivial.

**ADVANCE when:** the team has selected a defensible concept and knows exactly what it must learn next.

**Mental test:** Explore → Compare → Decide → What could prove us wrong?

## Stage 4 — ITERATE

**Required Artifact:** Tested Prototype + Test Plan + Iteration Log

**Submission format:** Prototype or appropriate testable representation (software, physical, simulated, process-based, service-based, research-based, visual, or another appropriate representation), plus Test Plan + Iteration Log.

**Must include:**
- Critical assumption being tested
- MVP / Minimum Testable Version
- Why the chosen prototype fidelity is appropriate
- Test question
- Success criteria defined before testing
- Test participants/context
- Permissions where necessary
- Observations
- Participant/user feedback
- Failed, partial, surprising, or inconclusive findings
- What was learned
- V1 → V2 changes
- Reason for every meaningful revision
- Evidence of retesting
- HS where applicable: Formal Experiment Design
- Technical projects where applicable: Git/repository + README

**Minimum evidence standard:** Students cannot advance with "people liked our prototype." There must be an actual test tied to the critical assumption, and: Test → Learn → Revise → Retest.

**Reviewer checks:**
1. Does the MVP actually test the critical assumption?
2. Was success defined before testing?
3. Was the test appropriate?
4. Are observation and opinion separated?
5. Did the team report inconvenient results honestly?
6. What did the team learn?
7. Did evidence cause an actual revision?
8. Was the revised version tested again?
9. Can the reviewer follow the V1 → V2 learning trail?

**REVISE when:** testing is informal, success criteria were created afterward, only positive feedback is recorded, changes are cosmetic/unrelated to evidence, or the revised solution was never retested.

**ADVANCE when:** the team has demonstrated a real evidence-driven iteration cycle.

**Mental test:** Assumption → Build → Test → Learn → Change → Retest

## Stage 5 — IMPACT

**Required Artifact:** Impact Report

**Submission format:** One polished Impact Report, approximately 4–8 pages or equivalent evidence package.

**Must include:**
- Impact statement
- Success metric
- Why that metric matters
- Baseline, benchmark, comparison condition, or other appropriate reference point
- Result
- Quantitative evidence where appropriate
- Qualitative evidence where appropriate
- Data interpretation
- Alternative explanations
- Responsible claims
- Limitations
- Unintended consequences
- Impact across relevant stakeholders
- Cost vs. benefit
- Final conclusion: Supported / Partially supported / Not supported
- HS/advanced where applicable: Adoption/Uptake, Scalability, Leading/lagging indicators, ROI/unit economics, Advanced analysis

**Minimum evidence standard:** Impact cannot mean "we finished the solution" or "people liked it." Students must demonstrate a meaningful before/after, benchmark, comparison, or other credible evidence of change appropriate to the innovation.

**Reviewer checks:**
1. Does the metric actually relate to the original problem?
2. Is there a credible baseline, benchmark, or comparison condition?
3. What changed?
4. How was it measured?
5. Are claims proportional to the evidence?
6. Are alternative explanations acknowledged?
7. Are limitations clearly stated?
8. Were unintended consequences investigated?
9. Did different stakeholders experience different outcomes?
10. Was the benefit worth the cost/resources?
11. Does the final conclusion match the evidence?

**REVISE when:** there is no credible reference point (baseline, benchmark, or comparison condition), metrics do not represent the problem, claims are exaggerated, limitations are hidden, or evidence is selectively reported.

**ADVANCE when:** the team can state honestly what changed, what did not, and what the evidence actually supports. Importantly: a null or negative result can advance. A project does not need to prove the solution worked — it needs to prove learning with credible evidence.

**Mental test:** Compared to what? → What changed? → How do we know? → What didn't we prove?

## Stage 6 — INFLUENCE

**Required Artifact:** Final Pitch + Innovation Portfolio

**Submission format:** Final Innovation Portfolio, plus Final Pitch/Presentation, plus appropriate solution demonstration.

**Must include:**
- Audience definition
- Evidence-based story
- Clear problem
- Research evidence
- Solution selection reasoning
- Prototype/iteration story
- Impact evidence
- At least one strong evidence visual
- Appropriate demo
- Major claims linked to evidence
- Limitations
- Implementation & Sustainability Model
- Clear next-step Ask
- Reflection
- Future direction
- Mock Reviewer Q&A completion
- HS: Budget/resources, Funding/support model where applicable, Partnerships, Implementation risks/mitigation, Executive Summary
- Stretch: Go-to-market/adoption, Opportunity sizing, Technical architecture/deployment

**Minimum evidence standard:** A polished deck alone is not enough. Students must demonstrate that they understand and own the complete journey.

**Reviewer checks:**
1. Can the team explain the problem clearly?
2. Does every major claim trace to evidence?
3. Can they show what changed through the six stages?
4. Can they demonstrate the solution appropriately?
5. Can they explain failures and limitations?
6. Can they answer difficult questions without bluffing?
7. Is implementation realistic?
8. Is sustainability considered?
9. Is the Ask specific?
10. Does every team member understand the project?
11. Can every team member explain their contribution?

**REVISE when:** the pitch relies on hype, major claims lack evidence, students cannot defend their work, one member appears to own the entire project, implementation is unrealistic, or the Ask is unclear.

**FINALS READY when:** the team can make a credible, evidence-backed case for what should happen next.

**Mental test:** Explain → Show → Prove → Defend → Ask

## The Complete Six-Stage Arc

INSIGHT proves there is a problem → INVESTIGATE proves students understand it → IMAGINE proves the solution was thoughtfully selected → ITERATE proves the solution was tested and improved → IMPACT proves what actually happened → INFLUENCE proves students understand, can defend, and can move the work forward.

## Four Frozen Principles Across the Entire Competition

1. Evidence over hype.
2. Revision is expected, not punished.
3. A failed hypothesis or unsuccessful solution can still represent excellent innovation work if the learning is rigorous.
4. Judges evaluate the quality of the innovation journey, not simply how impressive the final product looks.

That fourth principle prevents a student with expensive equipment, advanced coding support, or a flashy product from automatically outperforming a student who demonstrated far stronger thinking, evidence, experimentation, integrity, and growth.
